## VII. LEGAL CLAIMS AGAINST MONITORS

### 1. Fraud on the Court

**Legal Standard:** A party commits fraud on the court when they deceive the court through conduct that undermines the integrity of the judicial process.

**Application:**
- Monitors represented to the court they were conducting oversight
- Monitors accepted payment for oversight services
- Monitors failed to perform any substantive oversight during helicopter operations
- Monitors failed to respond to documented civil rights complaints
- This deception undermines the entire consent decree framework

**Case Law Support:**
- *Deputy v. Deputy* (Del. Ch. 2024): Court removed trustee for bad faith and fraud on the court, finding that secret amendments and undisclosed conduct constituted judicial deception.
- *Urias v. Daniel P. Buttafuoco & Assoc.* (N.Y. 2024): Court upheld claims under Judiciary Law § 487 for attorney deceit intended to deceive the court.

### 2. Breach of Fiduciary Duty

**Legal Standard:** Court-appointed monitors owe fiduciary duties to the court and the public to faithfully perform their oversight functions.

**Elements:**
1. **Duty:** Monitors owed duty to court and public to conduct meaningful oversight
2. **Breach:** Zero monitoring activity during helicopter operations; 32+ days unresponsive
3. **Causation:** Failure to detect allowed surveillance to continue unchecked
4. **Damages:** Continued civil rights violations, County funds wasted

**Case Law Support:**
- *In re Caremark International Inc. Derivative Litigation*, 698 A.2d 959 (Del. Ch. 1996): Directors have fiduciary duty to monitor for fraud or illegal conduct. Monitors, as court-appointed fiduciaries, have even higher duty.
- *Abdou v. Mahany* (S.D.N.Y. 2021): Court addressed breach of fiduciary duty claims against attorneys for improper fee handling, emphasizing fiduciary obligations in qui tam context.

### 3. Professional Negligence

**Legal Standard:** Professionals (including court-appointed monitors) must exercise reasonable care and skill in performing their duties.

**Evidence of Negligence:**
- Failed to detect $12M helicopter purchase during oversight period
- Failed to respond to documented surveillance complaints
- Failed to update monitoring website for 3+ years
- Failed to produce any substantive findings during critical period

### 4. False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. § 3729)

**DOJ Civil Rights Fraud Initiative (May 2025):**
The DOJ has announced aggressive enforcement of the FCA against entities that "certify compliance with civil rights laws while knowingly engaging in [violations]."

**Application to Monitors:**
If monitors were paid from federal grant funds and certified compliance with oversight requirements, they may face FCA liability for:
- False certifications of oversight performance
- Accepting payment for services not rendered
- Material misrepresentations to the court

**Case Law Support:**
- *Universal Health Services, Inc. v. United States ex rel. Escobar*, 579 U.S. 176 (2016): Implied false certification theory applies when specific compliance representations are made while material non-compliance is concealed.

3. **Fraud on Court Theory:** Deliberate failure to perform oversight while accepting payment, coupled with concealment of non-performance, constitutes fraud on the court.

4. **FCA Exposure:** If monitors received federal funds and falsely certified performance, they face FCA liability under the implied false certification theory.
